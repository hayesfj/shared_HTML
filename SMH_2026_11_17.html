Interpretation Guide how to read every score, regime, signal and chart on the dashboard

While the market is open, tickers marked LIVE (●) are scored on the current intraday price — today's bar is still forming, so RSI, PPO, EMAs and the POS/NEG Signals will keep moving until the close. Pass --eod-only to score only confirmed closes instead.

Signal

  • Signal replaces the former Buy / Hold / Sell / Exit / Watch labels. It is a relabelling of the same alert logic: no score, threshold or gate changed.
  • Favorable (was BUY) — fresh PPO signal-line ↑ (histogram still positive) plus buy score ≥ the regime threshold, volume/range confirmed. RSI Extreme caps the POS Signals score one point below the regime threshold.
  • Unfavorable (was SELL) — fresh PPO signal-line ↓ plus sell score ≥ the regime threshold. A lone bearish cross still floors at 30 (review); expanding red hist or a zero-line ↓ is what should push it through.
  • Neutral, conflicted (was EXIT, amber badge) — a fresh ↑ and a fresh ↓ cross both fall inside the 3-bar window, both scores clear their thresholds and are within 10 points: a whipsaw, so de-risk.
  • Watch Favorable / Watch Unfavorable (was Watch BUY / SELL) — the buy (or sell) score is close to its trigger without a fresh cross, or a Favorable that a gate blocked (shown in the Blocked by column; see Gates below).
  • Neutral (was HOLD) — neither side dominates.
  • In Trend (its own column, new in v54) — Signal is an event: Favorable needs a cross within 3 bars, so it lapses to Neutral within days even when the move continues. In Trend carries the state forward: it opens on the Favorable alert and stays open until an Unfavorable / Conflicted alert or a close under the trailing stop (highest close − 3 × ATR since entry; it never moves down). The column shows sessions and return since entry; Stop shows the stop and its distance. It does not change Signal.
  • Suggested size (Ticker page and drawer) — 1% of the portfolio at risk ÷ the stop distance (3 × ATR%), capped at 10%. A calm name gets a larger size than a volatile one for the same risk. Change risk_per_trade_pct / max_position_pct to suit.

How the scores are built

  • PPO is the trigger; everything else is context. An alert needs a fresh PPO signal-line cross — the other components size the confidence but cannot create an alert alone.
  • Full component-by-component weightings, regime weights, thresholds and gates are in POS & NEG Signals below.
  • Alerts use the last confirmed close even when the dashboard shows the live bar. Percentiles are a rank across the names loaded on the same bar (no look-ahead).

Trailing/Emerging Momentum (formerly Regime)

  • What the percentiles mean (v54). Each input is ranked across every name loaded on the same date, so 80 means “stronger than 80% of the list today”. Returns are first divided by the name's own volatility (and the PPO histogram by its ATR%), so a volatile stock is not Strong or Weak just because it moves more. Before v54 the yardstick was SPY's own history. Because the rank is relative, about half the list is always above 50: in a falling market “Strong” means holding up best, not rising. The EMA200 gate and the Market gate supply the absolute test. Dates with fewer than 30 names, and the Currency group, still use the older anchor. RS Rating is the blended percentile as a whole number, 1–99.
  • Strong — both ≥ 55: trend plus acceleration.
  • Dormant→Waking — emerging leads trailing by 10+: coiled, early.
  • Mature/Cooling — trailing leads emerging by 10+: strong on paper, losing thrust.
  • Weak — both < 45.
  • Neutral — everything else.

Squeeze pillars

  • Foundation — compression length + a recent release.
  • Momentum — PPO histogram rising + DMI above floor.
  • Condition — healthy RSI + room below the 52-week high.
  • Confirmation — positive OBV slope + non-extreme momentum.

PMO (Ticker tab)

  • Carl Swenlin's Price Momentum Oscillator, drawn for reference only — it is not part of any score, alert or recommendation.
  • Sectors and the Six Pack have their own buttons on the Rotation tab and are not mixed into its other views.

Daily workflow

  1. Changes first — what flipped since yesterday and why.
  2. Market Health — the backdrop: how the S&P, equal-weight and Nasdaq are doing, how many of your stocks are above their averages, and the SPY / QQQ / RSP cards.
  3. Signals → Watch and Fresh cross — set-ups for tomorrow.
  4. Ticker — the full page for one name: charts with signal markers, why it scores what it scores, how it ranks against the universe, and how its past signals worked out. Shift+Enter in the search box opens it directly.
  5. Squeeze Full + Favorable = strongest long set-up; Dormant→Waking with a fresh ↑ = earliest.
  6. Check Health whenever a name looks odd — stale bars and short histories are listed there.

POS & NEG Signals — how the 0–100 confidence is built

Each score is the sum of its components in points, capped at 100 (the realistic ceilings are about 83 for POS and 81 for NEG). They are scored independently: a name can have a high POS and a high NEG at once, which is what the Conflicted rule looks for. The bars on the action cards and the Ticker tab show exactly these components.

POS Signals

ComponentPointsHow it is calculated
PPO cross0–35The trigger. 18 × freshness (1.0 on the cross bar, falling 0.2 per bar to 0 after 5 bars) + 8 if the histogram is positive and still rising (× max(freshness, 0.4)) + 9 for a zero-line cross ↑ within 3 bars, or + 4 if PPO is already above zero. That sum is multiplied by (1 + 0.5 × cross strength), the regime PPO weight and volatility reliability, then capped at 35.
RSI0–18Location, not a second oscillator. Adds (zone multiplier − 1) × max(PPO points, 12) plus a zone bonus of 6 Oversold, 4 Weak or 2 Healthy (bonus × regime RSI weight × reliability); negative results are clipped to 0. Zone multipliers: Oversold 1.15 · Weak 1.15 · Healthy 1.00 · Overheated 0.70 · Extreme 0.00. Overheated and Extreme therefore add nothing, and Extreme also caps the POS Signals score one point below the regime threshold.
EMA0–1212 × (number of EMA20/50/200 the close is above ÷ 3) × regime EMA weight. Halved if the PPO histogram is negative. Cut to 35% when below EMA200 (kept only in Dormant→Waking with a squeeze release and room to run). Cut to 60% when within 3% of the 52-week high with no squeeze release.
Persistence0–88 × trend persistence (lag-1 return autocorrelation over 60 sessions, floored at 0) × reliability. Only counts when momentum direction or the PPO histogram is rising.
Squeeze0–10Small add-on: +6 recent squeeze release, +3 PPO turning up, +3 room below the 52-week high, capped at 10. Cannot create an alert on its own.

Floors (both sides, v54). A fresh bullish cross that is still in force guarantees a POS score of at least 30, exactly as a fresh bearish cross guarantees a NEG score of at least 30. Both floors sit below every alert threshold. Before v54 only the NEG side had one, which tilted Net Signals negative. NEG floor. A fresh bearish signal-line cross that is still in force guarantees a NEG Signals score of at least 30 (shown with a ° and “floor applied”). The floor sits below the alert threshold, so the cross alone means “review”; one confirming factor — usually an expanding red histogram, a zero-line cross ↓, or the close below an EMA — tips it into Unfavorable.

Weightings by regime

Regime weights multiply the PPO, RSI and EMA components (1.00 = no change). Alert thresholds are also regime-specific: it is easier to buy in Strong and Dormant→Waking regimes and harder in Mature/Cooling and Weak ones, and the reverse for selling.

NEG “PPO (used)” applies the 0.85 floor on the sell PPO weight so a strong trend cannot fully mute a genuine bearish cross. Thresholds rise by 10 points when the name’s ATR exceeds 6% of price, because very volatile names produce noisier crosses.

Volatility reliability

Reliability = 1 − (1 − 0.45) × min(20-day annualised volatility ÷ 100%, 1), so it runs from 1.00 for a calm name down to 0.45 for anything at 100% volatility or more. From v54 one discount is used on both sides: every reliability-scaled component (PPO, RSI bonus, persistence / weakness) is multiplied by 1 − 0.65 × (1 − reliability), so the most volatile names keep 0.64 of their points on POS and NEG alike. Before v54 the buy side took the full discount and the sell-side PPO points a much lighter one, so volatile names leaned negative on Net Signals by construction.

What an alert must pass

  1. Score ≥ the regime threshold (table above, plus the ATR adjustment).
  2. A fresh PPO cross in the same direction within 3 bars, still in force (histogram on the same side of zero).
  3. Complete data: at least 300 bars of history and no missing core indicators.
  4. Liquidity: 20-day median dollar volume of at least $5M.
  5. Bar confirmation: the cross bar’s dollar volume is at least 1.25× its 20-day median, or it closed in the top third of its range (buy) / bottom third (sell).
  6. Conflicted override: if both a POS and a NEG setup pass (fresh ↑ and ↓ crosses both in the window) and the scores are within 10 points, the call is Neutral (shown with a Conflicted badge); otherwise the side matching the current histogram direction wins.

A name that scores within 8 points of its threshold but fails a gate (no fresh cross, thin volume, high ATR) is listed as Watch, not as an alert.

Gates — why a high POS Signals score is not always Favorable

Favorable needs more than a score above the threshold. After the score is calculated, a name has to pass a series of gates. If it fails one, Signal stays Neutral, but since v41 the name is also marked Watch and the reason is shown in the Blocked by column (Signals tab), in the W badge tooltip, on the Ticker page and on the Health tab. Several reasons can apply at once (shown joined with “+”).

How to read a blocked name

  • Only “Relative strength” or “SPY regime” — the stock itself looks fine (fresh cross, above EMA200) and is being held back by the market or by lagging SPY. These are the Candidates filter on the Signals tab: the likeliest names to graduate to Favorable if the market or the relative-strength picture improves.
  • “Below EMA200” — an early repair attempt inside a long-term downtrend. Treat with more caution; only the Dormant→Waking + full-squeeze exemption lets these through.
  • “No fresh cross” — nothing wrong; the trigger has not fired (or fired more than 3 bars ago).
  • Several reasons — the more gates a name fails, the less this is a timing problem and the more it is a quality problem.

Unfavorable-side filter

Switch: stronger_top_detection (on). An Unfavorable rating is turned back into Neutral when the close is still above EMA50 and momentum direction is positive — a pullback inside an uptrend rather than a top. Unfavorable ratings have no “Blocked by” label.

Live prices and alerts

While the market is open, alerts are pinned to the last confirmed close (the POS/NEG Signals, RSI and PPO still move with the live bar). The gates are applied to that pinned recommendation as well. Use the Health tab's Favorable ratings held back by a gate list for a ticker-by-ticker view.

Currency tab — a view on the US dollar

FXE (Euro), FXY (Japanese Yen), FXB (British Pound), FXF (Swiss Franc), FXA (Australian Dollar), FXC (Canadian Dollar) — plus UUP, the dollar itself, as a reference. These funds hold the foreign currency, so their price rises when that currency strengthens and the dollar weakens. The tab is a portfolio-hedge tool: it is not a view on the stock market.

  • Reading the signals. Favorable on a currency fund = the currency is strengthening (dollar weakening). Unfavorable = dollar strengthening. For UUP it is the other way round. The USD read column spells this out.
  • USD view. The headline is the average Net Signals (POS − NEG) of the currency funds, weighted by approximate US Dollar Index weights (FXE 57.6% · FXY 13.6% · FXB 11.9% · FXC 9.1% · FXF 3.6%; AUD and MXN are not in the index and are left out of the weighted figure). A weighted Net Signals of +15 or more reads “dollar weakening”, −15 or less “dollar strengthening”, otherwise no clear trend. If UUP points the same way as the currency funds, the card says it disagrees. EUR is more than half of the index, so FXE dominates the reading.
  • Hedge fit (ρ SPY). The correlation of daily returns with SPY over the last 60 sessions. Negative means the fund tended to rise when stocks fell, which is what a hedge should do (yen and franc often); positive means it moves with equities (AUD, CAD and MXN often), so it would add to equity risk rather than hedge it. ρ UUP shows how closely it tracks the dollar. Correlations change, so check them before sizing a hedge.
  • One bet, not eight. All of these are largely the same trade (dollar weakness), so signals across the group usually agree. Disagreement between them points to a currency-specific story rather than a dollar move.

What is different from the other tabs

  • Own percentile base. Momentum percentiles are measured against the Currency group's own pooled history (not SPY's). Currency funds move a fraction as much as stocks, so against SPY they would all sit near the middle and never reach Strong or Weak. Regimes and Mom % on this tab therefore mean “strong for a currency”, not “strong versus the S&P 500”.
  • Gates. The SPY-regime and relative-strength-vs-SPY gates do not apply. The below-EMA200 gate still does, so a Favorable rating on a currency in a long-term downtrend shows up as a blocked Favorable.
  • Liquidity floor. $1.0M of 20-day median dollar volume for this group (other tabs use $5M), because several of these trusts trade thinly. Funds that cannot be loaded, or that no longer trade, are listed on the Health tab and shown as “no data”.
  • Left out elsewhere. The group is not in Rotation → All, the regime buttons or Alerts only (it has its own Rotation → Currency button), the Themes aggregates, or the missing-from-Theme-file list.

The funds track spot exchange rates less fees. They are a signal and hedge-sizing aid built on the same momentum model as the rest of the dashboard; they are not forecasts of currency moves.

Current Themes tab — how the Theme Score is built

Each theme (and sub-theme) is scored 0–100 from its loaded tickers, primary and secondary assignments alike (the same membership the weekly report uses, so a name can sit in two groups; secondary names are marked). Sector, Six Pack, ETF and Country ETF funds are excluded. The score is display-only and feeds no alert.

  • Momentum (40%) — median Momentum percentile of the members.
  • Breadth (25%) — average of % above EMA200, % with a bull stack (20>50>200) and % with PPO above zero.
  • Signals (20%) — 50 + average Net Signals ÷ 2, so a theme full of Favorable-leaning names scores above 50.
  • Acceleration (15%) — 50 + 2 × the 5-session change in median Emerging percentile + 0.25 × the 5-session change in average Net Signals + 1.5 × median 20-day return vs SPY, capped 0–100.

Themes with fewer than 3 loaded names are greyed out as too small to rank. The regime is the usual trailing × emerging rule applied to the member medians. The score history is reconstructed by re-running the model over each past session, like the rest of the dashboard history. On Rotation, Themes and Sub-Themes plot one bubble per group at its median trailing / emerging percentiles, and the theme picker plots the individual tickers of one theme.

Weekly signal and “vs weekly”. Beside the daily score each group shows its weekly Theme report signal and stars. aligned = the daily regime supports the weekly signal; rebound = a weekly Early / Failed signal the daily trend has not confirmed (the weekly report measures 1-week rebound and breadth recovery, the daily model measures trend level); extended = a Mature weekly leader whose daily momentum is flat or cooling; conflict = the daily regime contradicts the weekly signal. Note that the daily regime “Mature/Cooling” is a different thing from the weekly “Mature Leadership”.

Prior Week Themes tab — the weekly theme signal

This tab shows the weekly theme report (Weekly_Theme_Signal.csv for sub-themes, Weekly_Theme_Signal_Themes.csv for themes) as it is: Signal, Stars (0-3) and the Theme / Sub-Theme Breadth figures are not recalculated here. Beside them sit the daily Theme Score and regime from the Themes tab and a “vs weekly” flag, so the two reports can be read together. Drill down Theme → Sub-Theme → Ticker; the tickers come from the theme membership file.

  • Early Rotation — a sub-theme that has just started to lead (3 stars).
  • Mature Leadership — an established leader.
  • Mixed / Consolidating — no clear direction.
  • Failed Rotation — a rotation that did not hold (0 stars).

vs weekly: aligned = daily regime supports the weekly signal; rebound = the weekly report sees a 1-week rebound / breadth recovery that the daily trend has not confirmed yet; extended = a mature leader whose daily momentum is flat or cooling; conflict = the daily regime contradicts the weekly signal. The daily regime “Mature/Cooling” (high trailing, lower emerging percentile) is not the same thing as the weekly “Mature Leadership”. ✓ theme marks a name that is Favorable / Watch Favorable inside an Early or Mature sub-theme; probe marks a Favorable name whose parent sub-themes are all Failed. The near-miss table lists Early / Mature names the daily model has not cleared yet. None of this changes any alert.

The tab badge counts the Early Rotation sub-themes. A banner appears if the file is missing or older than 8 days against the session date. Display only - nothing here feeds any alert or recommendation.
